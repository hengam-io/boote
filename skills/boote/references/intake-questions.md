# Intake questions — boote's clarification bank

When the founder brings a raw idea (`boote new`), ask **3 to 5 sharp questions** from this bank — no more in a single turn. Lead with the **🔒 blocking** questions: the ones that, if unanswered, make a meaningful critique impossible (they map to `[unknown — blocking]` dossier fields). Ask the rest in later `clarify` rounds.

> The principle: a question should pull out a hidden assumption, not push the founder into defending themselves. Tone: a hard, curious mentor — Socratic, not an interrogator. The questions are ordered by the **validation ladder** ([`methodology.md`](methodology.md) §3); you almost always start at rung 1.

## Rung 1 — Customer & problem 🔒 (the most important; start here)
- Exactly **who** has this pain? Name **one** beachhead persona — not "everyone" / "SMBs" / "consumers".
- How **acute** is the pain? How do they solve it today (even with a janky workaround)?
- How often do they feel it? (frequency — daily/weekly/rare)
- If it isn't solved, what do they lose (money / time / risk)?
- **Evidence:** how many of these people have you actually talked to, and what did they *do* about the problem (not what they said they'd do)? (If zero → that's the honest starting point; the next step is to talk to them.)

## Rung 2 — Why now
- What changed **recently** that makes this feasible or urgent now and wasn't true three years ago? (a new API/LLM leap, a behavior shift, a regulation, a platform)
- If there's no "why now," why hasn't anyone done this yet?

## Rung 3 — Value / why switch
- Why would the customer choose this over their current alternative (including "do nothing")?
- What's the "job" they're hiring it for? What makes them switch despite the friction/habit of the status quo?

## Why us — founder-market fit
- What expertise / asset / access do you or your team have that a random team doesn't?
- Is this real leverage, or just "we can do it too"?
- Does this build on your existing expertise, or enter a wholly unknown industry chosen because it "looks empty"? (the unfamiliar-industry risk)

## Rung 4 — Solution & the riskiest test 🔒
- What's the simplest version that proves the value? (not the full product)
- What's the single assumption that, if wrong, kills the whole thing? (the riskiest assumption)
- What's the **cheapest** way to test that assumption *before* building (a fake-door, a concierge, a Wizard-of-Oz)?
- What makes it hard to copy? (a moat, even a weak one)

## Rung 5 — Market & business model
- How does it make money? How much is the customer willing to pay (and what's the signal)?
- How big is the bottom-up market? (a customer count × a real price — a first guess; research sharpens it)
- Where do the first 10 customers come from?

## Risk, horizon & host fit
- What's the biggest reason this **fails**? (ask the founder directly)
- Where does it sit in the host's strategy/portfolio?
- What would put it in conflict with the host's principles / red lines?

## The goal of this round (ask every time)
- What do you want from this idea right now? (just think it through / take it to the board / make a go decision)
- Who is the audience for the next presentation? (board / experienced outsider / team)

## The office-hours re-anchor (for `clarify` on an existing idea, and `office-hours`)
Open any recurring round with the four office-hours questions ([`methodology.md`](methodology.md) §12):
1. What's the **one most important thing** right now?
2. What did you **learn from users** since last time — what did they *do*?
3. What's the **current riskiest assumption**, and how are you testing it **this week**?
4. Are you **default alive**?
