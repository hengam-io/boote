# Reply templates — the two emails `/reply` writes

Two skeletons: **`iterate`** (critique + go develop it further) and **`pass`** (critique + we won't invest). They are scaffolds, not forms — fill every placeholder with the *specific* idea, and cut anything that doesn't apply. The rules behind them live in [`reply-methodology.md`](reply-methodology.md).

> **Before filling:**
> - **Voice & language.** Honor the resolved `voice` slot (text written on the owner's behalf — match its tone and any hard language rules). Write in the **recipient's language**: match the language they pitched in; if unknown, ask the owner.
> - **No boote fingerprints.** No rubric scores, no `validation_stage`/`evidence_level`, no `continue`/`pivot`/`kill`, no mention of boote/the critic/rounds. Plain founder-facing language. (See methodology §10.)
> - **Sources are mandatory.** Every claim that drives a reason cites its exact source inline, and the "What this is based on" block lists them. Never invent a citation. (See methodology §5.)
> - **Redaction.** Nothing confidential per the host's `output_style` (internal financials, the hurdle, other ideas, portfolio strategy).
> - **Length.** A quick reply is 5–9 sentences. Compress.
> - **Draft only.** `/reply` produces the draft; the owner sends it.

## Template A — `iterate` (critique + develop it further)

```
Subject: <idea/working name> — my read, and what I'd go prove next

Hi <first name>,

Thanks for bringing <idea> to us — <one specific, genuine thing about it: the
wedge, the insight, the instinct you rated>. I've spent real time on it, so
here's my honest read.

I think there's something here, and that's exactly why I want to push on it
before we take it further — it's not a yes yet, it's "I'm interested enough to
spend attention." The thing standing between this and a real conviction is
<the single biggest gap — usually the riskiest assumption or the evidence gap>:
<one or two sentences, concrete, with the source if it's a fact —
"<finding> (<source>, <date> — <link>)">.

<Optional second reason, same discipline. Mark fact vs. opinion: "this part is
my judgment, not data — tell me if I'm wrong.">

So here's what I'd go do before anything else, and it's specific:
<the one concrete next step — e.g. "talk to 10 of <persona> about the last time
they actually hit this, and what they did about it; bring me five of those
conversations">. That's the thing that would move this for me — not a better
deck, that.

I'm often wrong about these, so push back where you think I've got it wrong.
Send me what you learn in <timeframe, e.g. 3–4 weeks> and let's go again.
<Optional genuine offer: an intro, a pointer.>

<sign-off>,
<first name>

—
What this is based on:
- <claim> — <source title>, <date> — <link>
- <claim> — <source title>, <date> — <link>
```

**Notes for A:**
- The email's gravity is the **ask** — the next piece of evidence to go get. Make it small enough to actually do, and (per boote's evidence gate) usually "talk to users," not "build."
- Be explicit that interest ≠ commitment, so "I'm interested" isn't misheard as "you're in."
- Keep the door genuinely open — there's a real next step and a real time to reconnect.

## Template B — `pass` (critique + we won't invest)

```
Subject: <idea/working name> — where we landed

Hi <first name>,

Thanks for trusting us with <idea> and for the work you put into it — <one
specific, genuine thing you rated, briefly>. I owe you a straight answer, so
here it is: we're going to pass on this.

The reason comes down to <one thing — or "two things">: <reason 1, concrete and
sourced — "<finding> (<source>, <date> — <link>), which means <the implication
that drives the no>">. <Reason 2 if there genuinely is one, same discipline.>
<Where honest, frame it as about the idea/market/timing, not the person; mark
any opinion as opinion.>

<Optional — only if there's a real, objective path back:> If <measurable
criterion — e.g. "you ever get to N paying users at price P", "you found a
second segment that 5x'd the reachable market">, I'd genuinely want to see it
again — that's not a polite maybe, it's a real bar.

I could be wrong here, and I'd be glad to be. <Optional genuine offer: an intro
to someone better suited, a useful pointer, well-wishing that isn't hollow.>
Either way, I'm glad you brought it to us, and I mean that.

<sign-off>,
<first name>

—
What this is based on:
- <claim> — <source title>, <date> — <link>
- <claim> — <source title>, <date> — <link>
```

**Notes for B:**
- Say the no **early and plainly** — don't bury it. One to three sourced reasons, then stop; don't relitigate every flaw you found.
- The re-open criterion is **optional and must be objective** — a measurable bar, never "maybe later." If there's no honest path back, leave it out and close warmly instead of faking one.
- If the pass is a **host red-line / principle conflict** (from boote context), name it plainly and kindly as a *fit* decision, not a judgment of the idea's merit — e.g. "this sits outside what we back, full stop, and that's on us not you."
- Protect the relationship: a clean, kind no today is goodwill, deal flow, or a referral tomorrow.

## The "What this is based on" block (both modes)

- List the **2–4 sources** behind the claims that actually drove the verdict — title + date + link, pulled from the idea's `research/*.md` Sources lists (the original external URLs) or other cited material. (See methodology §5.)
- If a key point is the owner's judgment with no hard source, it does **not** go in this list — and in the body it is labeled as judgment, not evidence.
- If the recipient can't open internal links, use the public source URLs directly.
- Never list a source you didn't actually use, and never invent one to look rigorous.
