# Founder brief template — the one file `distill` leaves behind

`distill` collapses an idea into this single document, written in the **founder's own voice**, then deletes everything else. It is the only survivor, so it must stand entirely on its own: no links to rounds/research/economics (they are gone), every key number inlined with its source + date.

> **Voice:** first person — the founder talking. Honor the host `voice` slot if resolved; else confident, plain, and honest. English, no emoji. Aim for about one page — long enough to be real, short enough to read in two or three minutes.
>
> **Faithful, not polished-over:** represent the idea as last concluded, *including the parts that are still shaky*. The owner asked for the real idea "even if it has problems." Do not inject the mentor's fixes as if they were already solved, and leave out every trace of the critique — no scores, no stage/evidence labels, no verdict, no mention of boote.

---

```markdown
# <idea name or working title> — <one-line pitch / tagline>

## The idea
<2-4 sentences: what it is, in plain words, plus one concrete example of it in action.>

## Who it's for
<The one specific customer — a named persona in a specific situation, not "everyone" / "SMBs" / "consumers".>

## The problem
<The pain I'm going after: how sharp, how often, what it costs them today. And what they do instead right now — their current workaround.>

## How it works
<The simplest version that actually delivers the value. Not a feature list — the core of how the customer gets the win.>

## Why now
<What changed recently — tech, behavior, regulation, cost — that makes this possible or urgent now and not two years ago.>

## Why me / why us
<The unfair advantage or founder-market fit: what I understand, can build, or can reach that most others can't. Honest — if it's thin, say so.>

## The alternatives
<What they use instead today (including "do nothing"), the competitor or substitute I'd worry about most, and what I think I see that they don't.>

## How it makes money
<The model, the price, and the willingness-to-pay signal I have so far. If I've sized it, a bottom-up number (customers x price) with source + date. If I haven't, say so.>

## What I have so far
<The honest evidence: who I've talked to and what they actually did, any demand signal, a key number with its source + date. If it's still mostly an assumption, say that plainly.>

## What I'm still figuring out
<My own open questions and the biggest risks — in my words. The thing that, if it's wrong, breaks this. This is where the rough edges live; don't hide them.>

## What I'd love your take on
<The specific reactions or help I want from whoever reads this: a gut check, an intro, a "would you actually use this", a poke at the weakest part.>
```

---

Notes:
- **Self-contained.** Anything worth keeping from the research must be written into the brief itself, with its source and date — the research files do not survive.
- **No boote fingerprints.** No rubric, no `validation_stage`/`evidence_level`, no `continue`/`pivot`/`kill`, no "the critic said…". A reader should not be able to tell this came out of an incubation tool.
- **Re-ingestable.** The sections above deliberately cover what `boote new` asks for (customer, problem, why-now, why-you, how it works, alternatives, model, evidence), so the owner can drop this straight back into a fresh `boote new`.
- Skip or shorten any section that genuinely doesn't apply yet — but prefer an honest "I don't know this yet" over padding.
